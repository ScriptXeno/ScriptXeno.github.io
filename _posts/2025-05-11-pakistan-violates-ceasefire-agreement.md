# India-Pakistan Ceasefire Announced Amid Escalations and Violations

The fragile relationship between India and Pakistan took a pivotal turn on **May 10, 2025**, as both nations agreed to a [sudden ceasefire](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/10/india-and-pakistan-agree-to-an-immediate-ceasefire-after-days-of-escalation), bringing a brief pause to days of cross-border violence and political hostility. However, celebrations were short-lived as [explosions and violations](https://www.cnn.com/world/live-news/india-pakistan-operation-sindoor-05-10-25) were reported just hours after the agreement took effect.

## Ceasefire Agreement Announced

![LOC](https://scriptxeno.github.io/2025-05-11-pakistan-violates-ceasefire-agreement-images/loc.webp){:.shadow}

In a surprising development, military leaders from both India and Pakistan agreed to an **immediate halt to all hostilities** along the disputed **Line of Control (LoC)**. The ceasefire, which started at **17:00 IST (11:30 GMT)**, was the result of international diplomatic pressure.

> _"Pakistan and India have agreed to a ceasefire with immediate effect. Pakistan has always strived for peace and security in the region, without compromising on its sovereignty and territorial integrity!"_ said Pakistani Foreign Minister [Ishaq Dar](https://x.com/MIshaqDar50/status/1921175494633943541).

## Violations Rock Kashmir

Despite the agreement, explosions were heard across Srinagar, Baramulla, and Jammu, according to [reporting from CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/live-news/india-pakistan-operation-sindoor-05-10-25) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/live-blog/india-pakistan-rcna206000). Local sources reported projectile activity, a power shutdown, and air sirens in parts of Indian-administered Kashmir.

> _"What the hell just happened to the ceasefire? Explosions heard across Srinagar!!!"_ posted [Omar Abdullah](https://x.com/OmarAbdullah/status/1921224682159661251), Chief Minister of the federal territory, on X (formerly Twitter).

## Conflicting Statements Emerge

Indian Foreign Secretary [Vikram Misri](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/10/india-and-pakistan-agree-to-an-immediate-ceasefire-after-days-of-escalation) accused Pakistan of violating the agreement within hours, while Pakistan's Information Minister [Attaullah Tarar](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/14/did-pakistan-shoot-down-five-indian-fighter-jets-what-we-know) denied any such breaches, telling Geo News that "as of now, there have not been any ceasefire violations." The contradictory statements have fueled uncertainty over the truce's future.

> _"For the last few hours there have been repeated violations of the understanding arrived at earlier this evening between the directors general of military operations of India and Pakistan. We call upon Pakistan to take appropriate steps to address these violations,"_ said Misri, adding that Indian forces had been instructed to respond "strongly."

## Mediated by International Powers

The ceasefire was reportedly mediated by **multiple international actors**. U.S. President [Donald Trump](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/10/india-and-pakistan-agree-to-an-immediate-ceasefire-after-days-of-escalation) announced it first on Truth Social, saying: "After a long night of talks mediated by the United States, I am pleased to announce that India and Pakistan have agreed to a full and immediate ceasefire."

According to a [statement from the U.S. Department of State](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2025/05/announcing-a-u-s-brokered-ceasefire-between-india-and-pakistan), Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the two governments had agreed not only to the ceasefire but to start talks on a broader set of issues at a neutral site, praising both prime ministers' "wisdom, prudence, and statesmanship." India's own Ministry of External Affairs, notably, downplayed U.S. involvement and said no decision on further talks had actually been made, a discrepancy that itself became part of the story.

## Underlying Issues Still Simmering

![Image of Region](https://scriptxeno.github.io/2025-05-11-pakistan-violates-ceasefire-agreement-images/pak-tank.webp){:.shadow}

Although the ceasefire brought hope, key issues remained unresolved:

- The Indus Waters Treaty, suspended by India in April 2025 after an attack that killed 26 civilians in Indian-administered Kashmir, remained a point of contention. Pakistan's National Security Committee had warned that any diversion of Pakistan's water would be "treated as an act of war."
- Trade and visa restrictions imposed after April 22 were still in effect.
- Residents on both sides of Kashmir remained fearful of a relapse into violence.

> _"We want peace and an end to all these hostilities,"_ said Rumaisa Jan, a Srinagar resident.

## A Region Caught in Crossfire

In Pakistan-administered Muzaffarabad, locals welcomed the ceasefire with hope.

> _"For us, peace means survival,"_ said Zulfikar Ali. _"We've suffered enough."_

Security analysts warned at the time that the situation remained fragile, pointing out that the 2021 ceasefire, reinstated in 2023, was already under strain before the May 2025 escalation. Regional policy analysts, including South Asia specialists at the Stimson Center, made similar points in the days that followed: the underlying political disputes between India and Pakistan, not just the military posture along the LoC, were what needed addressing for the truce to hold.

## Final Thoughts

![Final Thoughts](https://scriptxeno.github.io/2025-05-11-pakistan-violates-ceasefire-agreement-images/cf.webp){:.shadow}

The May 10, 2025 ceasefire held past its shaky first hours, but it did not resolve the underlying disputes. The Indus Waters Treaty suspension, the trade and visa restrictions, and the deeper political disagreements between the two nuclear-armed neighbors all remained unsettled in the ceasefire's immediate aftermath, a reminder that a halt to shelling and a lasting peace are not the same thing.
