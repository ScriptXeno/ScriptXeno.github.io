Back in May 2025, this post covered Von.dev as a promising new entrant in browser-based AI coding: a free, VS Code-style IDE that ran entirely in the browser, with GPT-4o built in and support for bringing your own OpenRouter API key. It was in early beta at the time, sign-up was gated by email, and the team was actively shipping small updates like cheaper model costs and bug fixes.

That's not where things stand anymore. As of today, visiting https://von.dev/ returns a 404. This post has been rewritten to reflect that, rather than left up as a glowing review of something that no longer appears to exist.

## What we could confirm

Rather than guess, here's what's actually checkable right now:

- The site returns a 404 on the homepage and every path we tried.
- The domain's DNS is still live and points to a subdomain on Railway, a cloud hosting platform, rather than being unregistered or parked. So the domain itself hasn't lapsed, but whatever application used to run there is gone.
- The Wayback Machine's last successful capture of the Von.dev homepage is dated September 27, 2025. After that there are no further successful captures in its index, only a single redirect capture in mid-October 2025 before the trail goes cold.
- We could not find an official shutdown announcement anywhere: no blog post, no X or Twitter statement, no archived Discord message, nothing on Hacker News or Product Hunt. If the Von.dev team ever explained what happened, we couldn't locate it.
- A handful of "AI app builder" review and alternatives roundup sites still describe Von.dev as an active product, complete with named pricing tiers. Given the domain has 404'd for the better part of a year with no primary source saying otherwise, that reads like stale or auto-generated content that never got updated, not evidence the product is still running.

The honest summary: Von.dev appears to have gone offline sometime after September 2025, with no public explanation we could find. If that changes, or if it resurfaces under a new name, we'll update this post.

## What Von.dev was, briefly

For context on what this post used to recommend: Von.dev was a browser-based development environment styled after VS Code, aimed at full-stack work across a React frontend and a Python or Node backend. The pitch was AI-assisted coding, autocomplete, real-time debugging help, and code suggestions, powered by GPT-4o. You could plug in your own OpenRouter key so you controlled which model you were actually paying for, and it supported connecting external data sources like Google Sheets for quick dashboards or CRM-style builds.

All of that was free, sat behind an early-access signup, and was explicitly labeled beta. None of it is available anymore that we could verify.

## FAQ

### What happened to Von.dev?

We don't know for certain. The site returns a 404 today, and its last successful archived snapshot is from September 27, 2025. We found no official statement explaining why or when it went offline.

### Is Von.dev coming back?

Unknown. Its domain is still registered and still points to hosting infrastructure, so it hasn't simply expired, but there's currently nothing being served there.

### Was Von.dev ever really free?

Yes. During the beta period this post originally covered, in May 2025, it was free to use with your own OpenRouter key. That's moot now since the product isn't reachable.

### Can I recover projects I built on Von.dev?

Not through the tool itself, since it's offline. Any local exports or copies of generated code you kept aren't affected by this. There's no known way to reach an account or workspace through the current 404 page.

### What should I use now instead of Von.dev?

See the alternatives above. The [free AI coding tools roundup](https://scriptxeno.github.io/posts/best-free-ai-coding-tools-2026-solo-developers/) is the best general starting point.

### Should I trust sites that still list Von.dev as an active tool?

Be skeptical of them. We found several "alternatives" and "review" pages dated 2026 that describe Von.dev as though it's currently running, down to specific pricing tiers. None of that matches what we could verify directly, and none of those pages cite a primary source. It reads like recycled or auto-generated content that was never updated after the product went dark.
